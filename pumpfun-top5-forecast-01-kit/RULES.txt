Predict the top 5 new Solana coins by market cap, 24 hours out

## The task
Forecast the 5 largest pump.fun coins by market cap at each of the house's seven answer reads, an hour apart from 18 to 24 hours after the opening: the reads that start at 2026-11-12T20:00:00Z, 21:00, 22:00, 23:00, 00:00, 01:00 and 2026-11-13T02:00:00Z, among the coins launched at or after the opening, 2026-11-12T02:00:00Z. Your program, `node /bot/main.ts` (Node 22) or `python3 /bot/main.py` (Python 3.13) at the root of your repository, reads the house's 10-minute snapshots of those coins at /input and writes one file, /output/forecast.json: for each of the 5 places, a probability for each coin you name being at that place, your top 5 in order, and for each place a probability for each bin its market cap may fall in. In the final the history ends at 2026-11-12T13:00:00Z, an hour before the deadline, 2026-11-12T14:00:00Z, the same for every entry and for the house model forecast. It runs in a sandbox with no network, so it cannot read the chain at the answer reads: it forecasts them. Your one forecast is scored at every one of the seven reads, and your score is the mean (Scoring).

## Which coins count
- A coin counts when the pump.fun program, 6EF8rrecthR5Dkzon8Nwu78hRvfCKubJ14M5uBEwF6P on Solana mainnet, created it with its CreateEvent's timestamp at or after the opening, 2026-11-12T02:00:00Z, and it is quoted in SOL: its create's quote mint is 11111111111111111111111111111111, native SOL.
- Mayhem-mode coins count once their pool prices them: a mayhem coin still on its bonding curve is not ranked, and a forecast naming one the history never ranked is dropped like a coin born after the cut. A mayhem coin (one created in pump.fun's Mayhem Mode, its curve's or pool's is_mayhem_mode set) whose curve has completed but whose pool is not created yet is not ranked either. On its curve a mayhem coin's price stands on virtual reserves with almost no SOL behind them, so until it graduates to its canonical PumpSwap pool it holds no rank in any snapshot or in the answer, and it is still counted in each snapshot's `eligible` and `priced`. The chart therefore leaves out coins that pump.fun's own website lists among the largest: a mayhem coin on its curve is not on it, however large its price makes it there. Once its pool prices it, it is ranked like any other coin.
- A coin quoted in USDC or in another coin does not count, in this first contest: the house leaves it out of every snapshot and the answer, and counts it in each snapshot's `excluded_non_sol`. A coin created before the opening never counts, however large it is.
- Coins launched after the history ends count as much as the others: one launched at 2026-11-12T22:00:00Z that is the largest at a later answer read takes place 1 there. See Coins born after the cut.

## Market cap
- A coin's market cap is in lamports (10^9 lamports are 1 SOL), its price per raw unit times its own total supply S in raw units (6 decimals): S = 10^15 for an ordinary coin, pump.fun's 1 billion tokens, and S = 2 x 10^15 for a mayhem-mode coin, whose 2 billion are pump.fun's 1 billion plus the 1 billion its Mayhem Mode agent mints at creation (pump.fun's Mayhem Mode docs: 2,000,000,000 total token supply). S is fixed by the kind of coin, whatever its mint account's supply reads later (a burn does not shrink it). The cap is rounded down to a whole lamport, and it is never taken from the mint's supply, another pool, a trade event's reserves, pump.fun's own website or API, or any price vendor.
- Each coin is priced from its own accounts, read together in one getMultipleAccounts call from a Solana mainnet RPC node, so every figure comes from one slot. A row's `venue` says which formula priced it:
- `curve`, a coin still on its bonding curve: floor(virtual SOL reserves x S / virtual token reserves), both read from the bonding curve account.
- `pool`, a coin that has graduated to its canonical PumpSwap pool: floor((the pool's SOL vault amount + the pool's virtual_quote_reserves) x S / the pool's token vault amount). The canonical pool is the one pump.fun's migration creates, the program address ["pool", index 0 as a u16 little-endian, the coin's pool authority (the pump.fun program address ["pool-authority", mint]), mint, wrapped SOL] under the PumpSwap program pAMMBay6oceH9fJKBRHGP5D4bD4sWpmSwMn52FMfXEA; its two vaults are its associated token accounts, and virtual_quote_reserves (a signed 128-bit integer, which may be negative) is the pool's own field. No other pool of the coin is read.
- `migrating`, a coin whose curve has completed and whose pool is not created yet (no trade can run in that state): floor(quote x S / base), the price the pool will open at, where base is the token amount the curve's own token account holds and quote is the curve's real SOL reserves plus its post-completion SOL in, less pump.fun's pool migration fee (the reserves the migration deposits).
- Each row carries its `slot`, the context slot of the read it was priced from, so anyone can check it against the chain: /input/NOTICE.txt says how, through the transactions that set those accounts.
- A mayhem coin is priced on every venue, but only a `pool` row of one is ranked (Which coins count): a row of your history with `mayhem` true is always a `pool` row.
- A place's size is log10 of its market cap in SOL: log10(market_cap_lamports) - 9.

## Your forecast
/output/forecast.json, at most 1,048,576 bytes of UTF-8 JSON: one object with exactly three keys.
- `probabilities`: a list of rows, each exactly `{"mint": <string>, "place": <integer>, "p": <number>}`: a coin's mint address (base58, 32 to 44 characters), a place from 1 to 5 (1 the largest market cap), and the probability from 0 to 1 that the coin is at that place at an answer read (the one forecast is scored at every read). A coin may be named at several places, at most once at each, and a place has at most 100 rows. Each place's probabilities add up to at most 1. What a place leaves unnamed (1 minus its sum) is shared equally by every coin on the answer's chart that you did not name at that place; a place you name no coin at is spread evenly over the chart. Each place is scored on its own, so nothing limits a coin's probabilities over the five places, though an honest forecast's add up to at most 1: a coin holds one place at a time.
- `top5`: exactly 5 distinct mint addresses, your best guess at the top 5 in order. It does not change your log score: it gives the tie-break.
- `sizes`: a list of rows, each exactly `{"place": <integer>, "bin": <integer>, "p": <number>}`: a place from 1 to 5, a size bin from 0 to 81, and the probability that the market cap at that place, whichever coin holds it, falls in that bin at an answer read. The 82 bins are of log10 of the market cap in SOL: bin 0 holds a cap below 10^2.00 (100 SOL), bin i from 1 to 80 a cap from edges[i-1] up to but not including edges[i] (each a twentieth of a power of ten: bin 21 is 10^3.00 up to 10^3.05, 1,000 up to 1,122 SOL), and bin 81 a cap of 10^6.00 (1,000,000 SOL) or more. A place may be named at several bins, at most once at each, and its probabilities add up to at most 1; what a place leaves unnamed is shared equally by its bins you did not name.
A mint a row names need not be on the answer's chart: a row on a coin that is not there spends its probability for nothing. A mint that no line of your history names is not scored at all: see Coins born after the cut. For example `{"probabilities": [{"mint": "AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAApump", "place": 1, "p": 0.5}, {"mint": "BBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBpump", "place": 1, "p": 0.2}, {"mint": "BBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBpump", "place": 2, "p": 0.4}], "top5": ["AAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAAApump", "BBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBBpump", "CCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCCpump", "DDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDDpump", "EEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEEpump"], "sizes": [{"place": 1, "bin": 26, "p": 0.3}, {"place": 1, "bin": 27, "p": 0.3}]}` gives coin A 0.5 and coin B 0.2 to be the largest at each read and coin B 0.4 to be the second, spreads places 3 to 5 evenly over the chart, gives place 1's market cap 0.3 each on 10^3.25 up to 10^3.30 SOL (1,778 up to 1,995 SOL) and on 10^3.30 up to 10^3.35 (1,995 up to 2,239 SOL), its other 80 bins sharing the 0.4 left (0.005 each), and spreads the other places' sizes evenly. Your program writes the file to $MAKEFUN_OUTPUT_DIR when that is set, else to /output/, and reads $MAKEFUN_INPUT_DIR when that is set, else /input/: the grader's container leaves both unset, and a run on your machine sets both (Entries, below). A file that is missing, too large, not JSON or not of this shape is no forecast: it scores the floor at every place and every size of every read and takes no prize.

## Scoring
- Why seven reads: one snapshot of the chain can be won by a fluke, or by a spray of entries that each bet everything on a different coin, one of which happens to land. So the one forecast you write is scored at each of the seven answer reads (The answer), an hour apart, and your log score is the mean of its scores there: the forecast that gets the order and the sizes right read after read wins, not the one that got lucky once.
- At each answer read the forecast gives two scores, each the natural log of the probability you gave what happened, floored at 0.0001, so an outcome you gave nothing scores ln(0.0001) = -9.210340, never minus infinity.
- The order score of a read: for each place k from 1 to 5, the probability you gave the coin actually at place k at that read (your row's p when you named that coin at place k; otherwise place k's unnamed share, 1 minus the sum of your place-k rows, divided by the number of coins on that read's chart you did not name at place k), its log floored, added up over the places: from 0 down to -46.051702.
- The size score of a read: for each place k from 1 to 5, the probability you gave the bin place k's market cap fell in at that read (your row's p, otherwise the place's unnamed share), its log floored, added up over the places: from 0 down to -46.051702.
- Log score (the rank): the mean, over the answer reads the answer holds (an instant with no read is left out: The answer), of each read's order score plus its size score, from 0 down to -92.103404. Higher is better and 0 is the best. It is written to 6 decimals, and the board ranks on those 6 decimals. In the public leaderboard's rows the log score is rounded to 3 significant figures (-40.408345 shows as -40.4); your own entries' rows, the result document and the archive keep it exact, and every rank, tie and prize is decided on the exact value. The board also shows the order score and the size score, each the mean over the reads, written to 6 decimals on its own.
- Top-5 agreement (the tie-break): at each read, for every coin in both your top5 and that read's top 5, 5 minus the distance between its two places (the same place scores 5, one place apart 4, and so on), added up: 0 to 25 a read, summed over the reads: up to 175 over all seven. Two exactly equal log scores are ordered by it, and exactly equal agreement too goes to the earlier receipt.
- A forecast that does not read ranks below every one that does and takes no prize.
- A worked example: say a read's chart lists 100 coins, its top 5 coins A, C, B, F and G, and place 1's market cap is 1,800 SOL (bin 26). The example forecast above scores ln(0.5) = -0.693147 at place 1 (coin A, named there); ln(0.6 / 99) = -5.105945 at place 2 (coin C, not named there: place 2's unnamed 0.6 shared by the 99 coins on the chart it did not name there); and ln(1 / 100) = -4.605170 at each of places 3, 4 and 5, which it named no coin at: an order score of -19.614603. Its sizes score ln(0.3) = -1.203973 at place 1 and ln(1 / 82) = -4.406719 at each of the other four places, which it named no bin at: a size score of -18.830850. It scores -38.445453 on that read, and its top-5 agreement there is 5 (coin A at place 1 in both) + 4 (coin B, one place apart) + 4 (coin C, one place apart) = 13.
- A worked mean: say the next read's top 5 is coins A, B, C, F and G, and place 1's market cap is 2,100 SOL (bin 27). The example forecast scores ln(0.5) at place 1, ln(0.4) = -0.916291 at place 2 (coin B, named there) and ln(1 / 100) at each of places 3, 4 and 5: an order score of -15.424948; its sizes score ln(0.3) at place 1 (bin 27, named) and ln(1 / 82) at the other four: -18.830850 again. It scores -34.255798 on that read, with an agreement of 15 (coins A, B and C each at its own place). Had the answer held only these two reads (the other five left out), the forecast's log score would be the mean of the two, -36.350626 (order -17.519776, sizes -18.830850), and its agreement 13 + 15 = 28; with all seven reads, the mean of all seven, and the agreement summed over all seven.
- Log scores reward honest probabilities: you score best on average by giving each coin, at each place, and each bin the probability you really believe. Leaving a coin unnamed at a place is not giving it 0: it takes an equal share of what the place leaves unnamed. A row with p 0 gives its coin 0 at that place (the floor if it does hold it) and takes it out of that share, so naming at p 0 the coins you are sure will not hold a place, up to the place's 100 rows, leaves that share to the rest of the chart's coins, the coins not yet born among them. A forecast that spreads every place evenly over the chart's 100 coins and every place's size evenly over the 82 bins scores 5 x ln(1/100) + 5 x ln(1/82) = -45.059447 at every read, so -45.059447 on the mean. Putting everything on one order and one bin per place (p 1) scores 0 only if every place and every bin is right at every read; each one missed costs 9.21 (it scores the floor, -9.210340), so such a forecast that misses 3 of the 5 places and 3 of the 5 bins at a read scores 6 x -9.210340 = -55.262042 there, far below the even spread.

## Coins born after the cut
- Coins launched after the history ends (in the final, from 2026-11-12T13:00:00Z to the last answer read) count, and they may take any place: nobody's history holds them, so no forecast can name one. A place such a coin takes is scored from the probability each forecast left unnamed there, shared by the chart's coins the forecast did not name at that place: the same rule for every entry, and the reason to leave part of each place unnamed when you think a newcomer may take it.
- A forecast is scored on the coins its history shows alone: a `probabilities` row naming a mint that no row of /input/history.ndjson names is dropped before scoring, and its probability goes back to that place's unnamed share, as if the row had never been written; a `top5` mint that the history never names holds no place in the agreement. A coin's creator can know its mint address before launching it, so no entry may name a coin it could not have seen in its history: such a row scores exactly what leaving its probability unnamed scores. The forecast still reads, and `sizes` name places, not coins, so nothing there is dropped. The answer file carries the history's mints (`history_mints`) for the scorer, which sees only the answer.
- A mayhem coin that was still on its curve through the whole history is treated exactly so: no row of the history ranked it, so a row naming it is dropped like a row naming a coin born after the cut, even when its pool prices it by an answer read and it takes a place there. That place is scored from the share the forecast left unnamed, as for a newcomer.
- In the worked example above, coin F at place 4 was born after the cut. Had the forecast also named it, say with 0.9 at place 4, that row would have been dropped and the forecast would have scored exactly the same.
- In the practice, 2 of the top 5 at its last read were launched after its history ended, and of the 9 coins that held a top-5 place at any of its seven reads, 3 were.

## Baseline and prizes
- The house baseline (starter/main.ts in the kit) forecasts that the order of the last mark holds: at place 1 it gives 0.30 to the coin then #1 and 0.10 to the coin then #2; at each place k from 2 to 5, 0.30 to the coin then at #k and 0.10 to each of the coins then at #k-1 and #k+1; it leaves the rest of every place unnamed (0.60 at place 1, 0.50 at the others), for the coins that move and the coins not yet born. Its sizes give each place's market cap a normal distribution in log10(cap in SOL) around the log10 of the cap then at #k, standard deviation 0.35, each bin's probability rounded down to 6 decimals; its top5 is the last mark's top 5. Its numbers were fixed in advance, never tuned on the practice. It scores -40.408345 on the practice forecast, the mean over its seven reads (order -21.380389, sizes -19.027956), with 56 agreement points summed over them; the kit's starter, the same program, scores the same. It is graded on the final like every entry.
- The house model forecast: the house also enters the forecast of an AI model (superbot, at reasoning effort max), made by one call the house pays for, given these rules, task.json, the forecast's format and the history's latest 12 marks, each mark's 30 largest coins (rank, mint, symbol, age, market cap in SOL and venue): once for the practice forecast, and once for the final on the final's history, which ends at 2026-11-12T13:00:00Z, an hour before the deadline, as every entry's does. Its answer is recorded, and every grading replays it byte for byte, with no new call.
- Ten places pay $200 in USDC: $50 for 1st place, $30 for 2nd, $24 for 3rd, $20 for 4th, $16 for 5th and $12 each for 6th to 10th, one place per entrant. Places go by log score, highest first, so 1st place is the best forecast that beats the best house entry. Beating the best house entry makes a forecast eligible, not a winner: when more than ten forecasts beat it, only the ten with the highest log scores take places. A prize needs a log score strictly above the best house entry's on the final: the house baseline's or the house model forecast's, whichever is higher (the house baseline's alone if the house model forecast was not entered). An entry at or below it keeps its rank but takes no place, and the next entry above it takes the place. The house baseline and the house model forecast rank on the leaderboard and take no place, since the bar is the higher of their scores and neither is above it; any other entry make.fun enters itself ranks like any other entry and must beat them too. A house bot that finishes in a paid place holds that place, and that place's prize is not awarded; no entry moves up to take it. A place no entry is left to take is not awarded. Exactly equal scores go to the earlier receipt after the tie-break.

## The command line
cli/makefun.mjs is the makefun command line in one file, everything it needs inlined: it runs on Node 22 or later with nothing to install. kit.json lists its sha256 and size, as it does every file's here. Run it from this directory:

  node cli/makefun.mjs --help

Its commands talk to https://make.fun, the site this kit was downloaded from (cli/site.json names it), with no --origin and no register needed for that. The origin is --origin, else $MAKEFUN_ORIGIN, else the one register stored in ~/.makefun/credentials (the site you last registered with), else https://make.fun. The API key is --api-key, else $MAKEFUN_API_KEY, else the one register stored for that origin.

## Grade locally
This is a program/v1 competition: its scorer and reference data stay on the server, which grades every entry; input/ holds the input data the entry reads. kit.json lists the sha256 and size of every file here.

## Secrets (optional)
Secrets are never required: an entry whose entrant pushed none is taken and graded like any other.

If your entry needs one, put it in a .env file as a NAME=value line and push the file to this competition, signed in (Enter, step 2), from this directory:

  node cli/makefun.mjs secrets push pumpfun-top5-forecast-01 --env-file .env

--stdin in place of --env-file reads the lines from standard input, and --dry-run prints what it would send (each name, its lane and size, never a value) and sends nothing. A value never goes on a command line, which a shell's history and an agent's transcript keep, and no MCP tool takes one. A push sets every name it holds and leaves your other secrets as they are: at most 16 secrets, each value 8 characters to 8192 bytes (a shorter value is too short to mask: keep it in your repository's config). PUT /api/competitions/pumpfun-top5-forecast-01/secrets takes {"secrets": {"NAME": "value"}} with your API key too. node cli/makefun.mjs secrets ls pumpfun-top5-forecast-01 shows what make.fun holds (names, lanes and hints, never a value), and node cli/makefun.mjs secrets rm pumpfun-top5-forecast-01 <NAME> removes one.

A value never belongs in your repository or your transcripts. make.fun publishes every entry's code with its whole history, so an entry whose repository holds a value you pushed, in any commit's files or message and in any encoding, is refused (committed_secret): rotate it, push the new value and purge the old one from the history. The check under Before you submit, run with --env-file .env added, looks there first for every form of each value of the file. A transcript holding one is masked before anything of it is stored, and never refused; a run whose outputs hold one (its state, output files or action log, scored byte for byte and so never masked) keeps its score, and make.fun withholds those outputs from every copy it publishes.

ANTHROPIC_API_KEY, OPENAI_API_KEY and GEMINI_API_KEY (or GOOGLE_API_KEY) are provider keys, which only an agent/v1 competition's model gateway uses: this competition runs no model gateway, so here they are stored and used by nothing, and never become an environment variable either.

Every other name becomes an environment variable of your entry at make.fun's runs of it, in every run of it a grade makes, and of nothing else: never the scorer or any program of make.fun's. An L2 regrade re-runs it with the same values while they are kept; once they are erased it is refused, and the L1 regrade, from the run's own record, stands. A reserved name (PATH, HOME, NODE_OPTIONS, LD_PRELOAD, PYTHONPATH, any GIT_*, MAKEFUN_* or MAKE_* name, and every variable the harness sets) is refused, and only a value's byte length is ever shown.

The sandbox has no network, so a secret for an outside service cannot be used: one serves only code that runs inside it, such as a key that decrypts a file your entry ships.

Each value is sealed to make.fun's grader when it is pushed, and erased once this competition's challenge window ends. A value you replace or remove reaches no run from then on and is erased at once, unless a final-phase run of your entry ran with it: make.fun keeps that one sealed until the challenge window closes, so a challenge can re-run that run, then erases it.

## The transcript gate
To keep every bounty fair, an entry is valid only with the transcripts of the agent sessions that built it, which show that the entrant's own agent built it: one without them is rejected, unless its competition makes them optional.

make.fun asks for the transcripts to keep every bounty fair: they show that the entrant's own agent built the entry, not someone else's work or existing open-source code passed off as theirs. The thinking checks match the code against the sessions that wrote it. They are never published, a winner's included: only the entrant's account and make.fun's admins can read them, and secrets, e-mail addresses, home folder names and usernames are stripped from them before anything is stored.

The instruction files and the system prompt are no part of this gate. An entry is never rejected for leaving them out. With no system prompt and no instruction file, bundle prints a warning and a reviewer sees a note that the instructions were not uploaded; the checks and the grade are the same.

This competition requires them: every entry uploads them, and they pass these checks before it is graded. Each is pass or fail, never a score: a fail rejects the entry with the check's fix, and a new entry can pass before the deadline. The checks this competition runs, and their thresholds:

  transcripts_present  No transcripts parsed
  thinking_wellformed  max_step_back_sec 60
  thinking_in_window   clock_skew_sec 60
  thinking_committed   Transcripts differ from the signed entry
  thinking_reproduces  min_reproduced 0.3 (30%); entrypoints main.ts, main.py
  thinking_turns       min_assistant_turns 5
  thinking_priced      min_priced_share 1 (100%); min_self_reported_usage_share 0.5 (50%); max_self_reported_tokens_per_sec 2000
  thinking_revised     min_file_writes 2
  thinking_fresh       min_fresh_share 1 (100%); clock_skew_sec 60

- thinking_wellformed: every user, assistant and tool message of a session carries a timestamp, no assistant or tool message falls more than 60 s behind the latest before it, and every tool result answers one earlier tool call of its session; a session file your agent exported itself names the harness that wrote it, in the file or with bundle's --harness.
- thinking_in_window: every transcript timestamp lies between the competition's opening and the entry's receipt time, give or take 60 s of clock skew.
- thinking_reproduces: the transcripts account for at least 30% of the counted lines (authored or pasted), and the entrypoint (main.ts or main.py) has at least one authored line.
- thinking_turns: at least 5 assistant turns carry text or thinking (a turn that only calls tools does not count).
- thinking_priced: every usage record prices to a known model; a self-reported session (a file exported afterwards, not a log a known harness wrote) carries the provider's usage on at least 50% of its assistant messages, with the provider's own token counts, not rounded or estimated figures, and its output is no more than a model produces in the time its own timestamps span (2000 tokens a second); an upload of harness-recorded logs with no usage has nothing to price.
- thinking_revised: some file (the entrypoint, or the code it runs) takes its final content from at least 2 assistant messages that write or edit it (a file written once in full and never revised counts 1).
- thinking_fresh: for an entry uploaded as a source bundle, every credited line comes from agent sessions that show the anchor line the kit's init prints (makefun anchor <root sha> round <round>) in a tool result before their first counted write (a Claude subagent launched after its parent's quote, or a Codex fork of a session that quoted it, counts with it); short of that the entry is not rejected, but its reward, if it places, is held for review, and a quote timestamped before the round fails.

## Claims and holds
A prize is a leaf of the payout root posted on chain, claimed by the winner to the payout wallet that signed the winning entry, with that wallet:

  node cli/makefun.mjs status
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --us-person <yes|no>

status lists your prizes to claim with their claim ids, and every prize of the account with how to claim it (the manual claim route or the claim command). claim asks the clearance route (POST /api/claims/<id>/clearance) to screen the claim, in order:

  claim_sanctions  A prize is not paid to a wallet on the sanctioned-address list the server screens claims against; the claim is held on chain.
  claim_geo        A prize is not paid to a claimant in a blocked country or region, or one whose country or region is unknown (the trusted proxy's country and region headers cannot place it); the claim is held on chain. Once the organizers' review releases the hold, a request whose country or region is still unknown passes, and one from a blocked country or region is held again.
  claim_tax_form   A US person whose prizes from this payer reach the reporting threshold in a calendar year files a W-9 before the prize is paid; non-US winners file nothing.

--us-person yes or no answers whether you are a US person (a false answer disqualifies); --tax-form <w9.json> files the W-9 when one is asked for. A cleared claim comes back as the claim transaction, co-signed by the compliance key, which pays its fee: claim checks that it claims exactly this prize and nothing else, signs it with the wallet and relays it (POST /api/claims/<id>/submit). A refused or held claim prints its problem: each failed check, its reason and its fix.

A hold is placed on chain, with its reason:

  sanctions          Wallet is on a sanctions list
  geo                Claim from a blocked or unknown country or region
  tax_form_required  W-9 required
  review             Held for the organizers' review: only their review releases it

A tax-form hold is released when the W-9 is filed with the claim; a sanctions or geo hold, only by the organizers' review.

If the clearance route does not answer, record the claim on chain yourself (the wallet pays the fee), then claim it once the hold window, 259200 s (72 h), has passed with no hold placed; the same command then claims on chain with no co-signature:

  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json --request
  node cli/makefun.mjs claim --claim <claim id> --keypair wallet.json

Claim before the claim deadline, 2592000 s (30 days) after the result becomes claimable: what is unclaimed then refunds to 7ycv2To2epAXAzyskv9KpXMS3Bez42q3oH9iMJk9ZRMY.

An account whose payouts are pinned to an owner's wallet W (delegate) does not claim: the prize pays W, which signs in with W (register --keypair <W's keypair>) and runs claim with its own keypair. The agent's own claim is refused, naming W.

## The harness
harness/harness-src.tar.gz (sha256 586afab52f79bafca0f0f0f409baa384863b3b1e53034c5bd35456aec5f012a7) holds the harness source exactly as this server runs it: every .ts, .json and .sh file of grader/, sim/, contracts/, pricing/, server/blobstore.ts and server/repos/index-tar.ts.
harness/SOURCES.sha256 lists every file's sha256 (`sha256sum -c` checks the unpacked tarball against it).
harness_version in kit.json is "harness@<digest> node <version>", where <digest> is the sha256 of the lines
"<path>\0<code sum>\n" for every file listed in harness/CODE.sha256, in that order. A file's code sum is the sha256
of its code (grader/version.ts codeSha256): a .ts file's text with its comments and the whitespace between its tokens
dropped (tsCode), a .json or .sh file's bytes. An edit to comments or whitespace alone names no new harness_version.
